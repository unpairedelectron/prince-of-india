# Historical references

How the game uses history: every story card and gallery entry carries one of three labels:
**HISTORICAL RECORD**, **TRADITIONAL ACCOUNT**, or **GAMEPLAY - FICTIONAL**.

## Facts used in Mission 1 and the gallery
| Claim in game | Label | Notes / sources |
|---|---|---|
| Pratap born 1540; birth associated with Kumbhalgarh | Historical (birthplace "traditionally associated") | Exact day differs between sources (9 May vs 18 May 1540), so the game gives the year only. |
| Accession as Maharana at Gogunda, 1572 | Historical | Sources give 28 Feb or 1 Mar 1572; the game gives the year only. |
| Father: Maharana Udai Singh II (d. 1572) | Historical | |
| Haldighati, 18 June 1576; Mughal army commanded by Raja Man Singh of Amber | Historical | The battle did not end the conflict. The game never calls it a Mewar victory, and Akbar does not appear in it. |
| Chetak carried Pratap from Haldighati and died | Traditional account | Labelled as tradition; any depiction will be non-graphic. |
| Dewair campaign, 1582, associated with Mewar's recovery | Historical | Pratap's forces took the Mughal post at Dewair. The game does not claim the conflict ended there, and does not claim Chittor was recaptured. |
| Chavand, a later centre of rule; death in 1597 | Historical | Day differs between sources (19 vs 29 Jan); the game gives the year only. |
| Kumbhalgarh is part of UNESCO "Hill Forts of Rajasthan" (inscribed 2013) | Historical | UNESCO World Heritage Centre, property 247: https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/247 |

## Facts used in Missions 2 and 3
| Claim in game | Label | Notes / sources |
|---|---|---|
| Imperial army commanded by Raja Man Singh of Amber; Akbar not present | Historical | Akbar never appears in Mission 2. |
| Mewar's forces included an Afghan contingent under Hakim Khan Sur (vanguard) and Bhil archers under Punja | Historical | Badayuni (an eyewitness, via secondary summaries) counts ~3,000 horse and ~400 Bhil archers under Punja; Hakim Khan Sur led the Afghan/Pathan contingent in the vanguard. Neither person appears as a playable or speaking character; allies in gameplay are composites. |
| Haldighati was not a Mewar victory; the imperial army held the field and Pratap escaped | Historical | Mission 2's goal is survival and withdrawal; the outro says the imperial army held the field. |
| "A contest of kingdoms and alliances, not of faiths" | Interpretation, supported by the composition of both sides | Hakim Khan Sur (Afghan) fought for Mewar; Man Singh (Rajput, Amber) led the imperial army. |
| Chetak carried Pratap out of the battle, across a stream, and died soon after | **Traditional account** | Contemporary sources do not name the horse. ASI's Chetak Samadhi page records the tradition (carried from Rakta Talai across the stream). Mission 3 is labelled as tradition; Chetak is never shown injured, and his ending is a calm resting pose and a fade. |
| Chetak Samadhi at Balicha near Haldighati; protected by ASI (monument of national importance, 2003) | Historical | ASI Jaipur Circle: https://asijaipurcircle.nic.in/ (Balicha - Chetak Samadhi). The memorial shown in game is a generic design, not a reconstruction. |
| Dewair 1582 (teaser only) | Historical | Mission 4 is not built yet. |

## Fictional content (labelled in game)
- Training ground, straw targets, sparring partners, wall course, approach course, final "simulated encounter".
- Chronicle Leaves (collectibles): game items, not artefacts.
- Mission 2: all soldiers (imperial and allied), the chokepoint "waves", the rear guard and the arrow-volley pattern are invented gameplay.
- Mission 3: the route, obstacles and their order are invented; only the stream crossing echoes the tradition.
- Saffron pennants are generic decoration, not reconstructions of a specific historical flag.
